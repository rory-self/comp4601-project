Sized vs whole-BO syncs — re-measurement of host<->kernel transfer overhead
mcoder K=8 ON FABRIC (KV260 PL @ 200 MHz), 64 KB kernel (bin/arith_64k.bin)

Follow-up to transfer_overhead_result.txt, which isolated the transfer
primitives and predicted that switching bo.sync(dir) -> bo.sync(dir, size, 0)
would save ~11.3 us per call at a 4 KB block.  This applies the change and
measures it A/B.

Method: src/size_bench.cpp gained a -w flag.  Default is now SIZED syncs;
-w reproduces the legacy whole-BO behaviour.  One binary, one board session,
alternating modes, so nothing else differs between the two arms.  28
configurations (2 sync modes x 2 block sizes x 7 payload sizes), every one
verified lossless -- the correctness pass runs in whichever sync mode is
selected, so a sized sync that failed to make the right bytes coherent would
show up as a round-trip mismatch rather than passing silently.

Raw data: results/sync_ab.csv


WHAT CHANGED IN THE CODE

  input:   bo_in.sync(TO_DEVICE)            ->  bo_in.sync(TO_DEVICE, n, 0)
  output:  bo_out.sync(FROM_DEVICE)         ->  bo_len.sync(FROM_DEVICE, 4, 0)
           bo_len.sync(FROM_DEVICE)             clen = hlen[0]
                                                bo_out.sync(FROM_DEVICE, clen, 0)

The output path has an ordering constraint the whole-BO version hides: you
cannot size the output sync until you know how many bytes the kernel produced,
and that length lives in its own BO.  So the length BO must be synced first.
That is two syscalls instead of one, and it still wins, because at this coder's
~38% ratio the output sync moves ~5x less data.

FINDING 1 — the prediction was right to within 3%.

transfer_overhead_result.txt predicted 11.3 us saved per call at a 4 KB block,
from the isolated primitives (whole-BO sync 5.60 + 9.41 = 15.01 us, sized sync
at 4 KB 1.88 + 1.83 = 3.71 us).  Measured here: 11.39-11.68 us across every
payload size, i.e. 11.5 us mean.  A prediction made from a benchmark that never
launched a kernel, confirmed against the full pipeline.


FINDING 2 — it is worth ~9% at a 4 KB block and ~0.6% at 64 KB.

That contrast is the whole point.  The saving is a fixed ~11.5 us per call
either way, but what matters is its size relative to the call:

  4 KB block:   11.5 us saved from a ~147 us call   -> ~9% faster
  64 KB block:   6.5 us saved from a ~1265 us call  -> ~0.6% faster

So sized syncs matter exactly where the buffer is oversized relative to the
payload, which is the same condition that made the transfer overhead look large
in the first place.  A design already using large blocks has almost nothing to
gain here -- it has already solved the problem a different way.


FINDING 3 — kernel-only throughput is unchanged, as it must be.

  block   4096: whole 31.10 M/s   sized 31.41 M/s   +0.98%
  block  65536: whole 49.10 M/s   sized 49.33 M/s   +0.47%

(means across all seven payload sizes; the 65536 means are pulled down by the
two rows that are really small calls.)  Sync happens outside the
enqueue+wait timing, so any difference here is run-to-run noise, and sub-1% is
consistent with the ~1-2% scatter seen throughout this project.  This is the
control: it confirms the change affects only the transfer path and did not
accidentally alter what the kernel does.


FINDING 4 — transfer overhead, restated honestly.

With sized syncs, host<->kernel transfer as a share of one call:

  block    transfer/call   kernel/call   share
   4 KB       8.4-10.4us      ~130us     6.1-7.4%
  64 KB      26.6-48.5us     ~1230us     2.1-3.8%

Compare the pre-fix figures, which is what size_sweep_result.txt reported:
13.4-14.1% at a 4 KB block.  Roughly half of that was the harness flushing
192 KB of buffer to move 4 KB of payload, not a real cost of using the
accelerator.

Note the sized 4 KB figure (6.1-7.4%) is still above the 3.4% that
transfer_overhead_result.txt computed from primitives alone.  The difference is
the per-call bo_len sync (~1.6 us of fixed syscall cost, unavoidable -- the host
must read the length back) plus the second syscall the sized output path
requires.  Both are real costs of the sized approach and are included above;
the primitive-level 3.4% excluded them.


FINDING 5 — normalised against the data itself: the interface is nowhere near
being the bottleneck.

A time or a percentage does not say whether the interface constrains anything.
Three normalisations against the actual data volume do.  All figures below are
for a 1 MB payload; clen is taken from each run's measured ratio.

(a) BYTES MOVED per BYTE PROCESSED.  The floor is 1 + ratio: you must move the
    input in and the compressed output out, and nothing less is possible.

      mode    block    processed      moved   amplification
      whole    4 KB        4,096    196,612       48.0x
      whole   64 KB       65,536    196,612        3.0x
      sized    4 KB        4,096      5,788        1.4x
      sized   64 KB       65,536     90,346        1.4x

    Sized syncs hit the 1.4x floor at both block sizes -- there is nothing left
    to remove.  Whole-BO syncs at a 4 KB block move 48 bytes for every byte
    coded, i.e. only 2.9% of the traffic is useful.  Aggregated over a 1 MB
    payload that is 50.3 MB of cache traffic to compress 1 MB, versus 1.48 MB
    once sized.

(b) RATE the interface sustains vs RATE the coder consumes.

      mode    block   transfer   moved B   xfer MB/s   coder MB/s   headroom
      whole    4 KB    20.95us   196,612       9,387         30.8      305x
      whole   64 KB    43.73us   196,612       4,496         53.3       84x
      sized    4 KB     9.81us     5,788         590         31.0       19x
      sized   64 KB    37.73us    90,346       2,395         53.3       45x

    Even after removing all the wasted traffic, the transfer path still moves
    data 19-45x faster than the coder can consume it.  The interface has an
    order of magnitude of spare capacity in every configuration measured.

    (The whole-BO rows show a HIGHER apparent MB/s precisely because they move
    more bytes per syscall -- cache maintenance is efficient per byte once
    started.  That is not an argument for doing it: those bytes are junk.  The
    sized 4 KB figure of 590 MB/s is low for the opposite reason -- three
    syscalls of ~1.6us fixed cost dominate a 5.8 KB transfer.)

(c) PER-BYTE cost, transfer vs compute (sized):

      block    transfer    coder      transfer as % of compute
       4 KB    2.39 ns/B   32.24 ns/B          7.4%
      64 KB    0.58 ns/B   18.75 ns/B          3.1%

    (The coder's ns/byte differs between the two because a 4 KB call still
    carries the full ~57us fixed per-call cost: 57.36/4096 + 17.89 = 31.9 ns/B,
    against 57.36/65536 + 17.89 = 18.8 ns/B.  That is the block-size effect from
    nsweep_32k_64k_result.txt, not a property of the transfer.)

The conclusion across all three: host<->kernel transfer is not a bottleneck and
was never close to being one.  It costs 3-7% of a call, moves at 19-45x the rate
the coder needs, and after sizing it is already at its theoretical minimum
volume.  There is no further optimisation available on this path.

Which puts the earlier result in its place.  This project's real data-movement
problem is INSIDE the kernel -- the byte-wide m_axi Split/Concat loops, measured
at 60-84% of kernel time (nsweep_32k_64k_result.txt, and the report's
"Where the runtime goes").  Those two things are both called "data movement" and
they differ by more than an order of magnitude in importance:

  host <-> kernel (this file)      3-7% of the call, 19-45x headroom
  kernel <-> DDR   (m_axi loops)   60-84% of kernel time -- THE bottleneck

Anyone reading "data transfer overhead" from this project should be told which
of the two is meant, because the honest answers are "negligible" and "dominant"
respectively.


WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT CHANGE

Changed: src/size_bench.cpp now defaults to sized syncs.  Every figure in
results/size_sweep_result.txt was taken with whole-BO syncs and is reproducible
with -w; its "+DMA" column is ~9% pessimistic at a 4 KB block and ~0.6%
pessimistic at 64 KB.  Its kernel-only column is unaffected.

NOT changed: src/bench_host.cpp and src/demo_host.cpp still use whole-BO syncs,
so every figure in onfabric_result.txt, nsweep_result.txt,
nsweep_16k_result.txt and nsweep_32k_64k_result.txt stands exactly as recorded.
Those are dominated by kernel-only measurements, which this change cannot
affect.  Applying the same fix to those hosts would improve their DMA-inclusive
numbers by the amounts above, and is left as a deliberate separate change so
that no already-published figure silently shifts underneath it.

Reproduce:
  ssh $BOARD 'cd /tmp
    XILINX_XRT=/usr ./size_bench_arm_64k -x <bin> -S 65536 -B 4096 -n 20      # sized
    XILINX_XRT=/usr ./size_bench_arm_64k -x <bin> -S 65536 -B 4096 -n 20 -w'  # legacy
