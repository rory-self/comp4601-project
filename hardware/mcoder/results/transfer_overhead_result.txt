Host<->kernel data-transfer overhead — mcoder on KV260, measured in isolation

Measured with src/transfer_bench.cpp, which launches NO kernel: it times the
transfer primitives alone, separating the three things the other hosts bundle
together.  64 KB kernel loaded (BOs: in 65536 B, out 131072 B), 2000 iterations
per point.


PLATFORM CAVEAT, WHICH FRAMES EVERYTHING BELOW

The KV260 is a Zynq UltraScale+ MPSoC: the PS (ARM) and PL (fabric) share the
same DDR.  There is no PCIe, no discrete device memory, and therefore no
host->device copy in the sense the question usually implies for an accelerator
card.  xrt::bo::sync() is CACHE MAINTENANCE -- flush before the PL reads,
invalidate after it writes -- not a bus transfer.

So "data transfer overhead to/from the kernel" splits into two very different
quantities on this platform:

  (a) making shared memory coherent, plus staging bytes into the mapped buffer
      -> what this file measures.  Small: 2-3.5% of a call.

  (b) the kernel's own m_axi traffic against DDR (the Split/Concat copy loops)
      -> the real data movement.  Large: 60-84% of kernel time, already
      characterised in nsweep_result.txt / nsweep_32k_64k_result.txt at
      ~2.6 cycles/byte.

If the question is "how much time goes to moving data rather than coding", the
answer is (b) and it dominates.  If it is "what does the host pay to hand data
to the accelerator", it is (a) and it is nearly free.


MEASURED (a): the transfer primitives, in isolation

  size(B)   memcpy    sync TO    sync FROM   sync TO    sync FROM   sized
            into BO   (sized)    (sized)     (FULL BO)  (FULL BO)   MB/s
  --------  --------  ---------  ----------  ---------  ----------  ------
      256    0.057us    1.680us     1.622us    5.604us     9.404us     77.5
     1024    0.207      1.714       1.656      5.582       9.399      303.8
     4096    0.795      1.880       1.826      5.575       9.393     1105.2
    16384    3.130      2.602       2.559      5.580       9.411     3174.5
    65536   13.968      5.509       5.471      5.598       9.413     5969.0

Three things fall out:

1. The FULL-BO sync columns are FLAT -- 5.6 us in, 9.4 us out, regardless of
   payload.  That is the cost of flushing/invalidating the whole 64 KB and
   128 KB buffers.  It is what every existing host in this project pays,
   because they all call bo.sync(dir) with no size argument.

2. The SIZED sync columns scale with payload, from 1.7 us at 256 B to 5.5 us at
   64 KB.  Cache maintenance has a fixed component (~1.6 us, a syscall) plus a
   per-byte one.

3. memcpy into the mapped BO is ~4.7 GB/s and is entirely avoidable -- an
   application that generates its data directly into the BO pays none of it.
   It is an application-structure cost, not an accelerator cost, and counting
   it as "transfer overhead" overstates the accelerator's tax.


WHAT THE CURRENT HOSTS ACTUALLY PAY, AND WHAT THEY COULD

Per kernel call, full-BO sync (current) vs sized sync (possible):

  payload   current: memcpy + 5.60 + 9.41   sized: memcpy + sized syncs   saving
    4096 B     0.79 + 15.01 = 15.80 us         0.79 + 3.71 =  4.50 us     11.3 us
   65536 B    13.97 + 15.01 = 28.98 us        13.97 + 10.98 = 24.95 us     4.0 us

As a share of the call (kernel time from nsweep_32k_64k_result.txt):

  payload   kernel   current transfer   sized transfer
    4096 B   127 us      15.8 (11.1%)      4.5 ( 3.4%)
   65536 B  1231 us      29.0 ( 2.3%)     24.9 ( 2.0%)

This reconciles the aggregate figure in size_sweep_result.txt.  There, DMA per
call measured ~20 us at a 4 KB block and ~33-48 us at 64 KB.  Predicted here:
4 KB -> 0.79 + 15.01 + bo_len sync ~= 17.4 us; 64 KB -> 13.97 + 15.01 ~= 29.0 us.
Both match.  The residual growth at large totals (33 -> 48 us) is the memcpy
source going cold: at a 16 MB payload the source buffer no longer fits in cache,
which is a property of the test harness, not of the accelerator.

So the honest figure for host<->kernel transfer overhead is 2-3.5% of a call,
NOT the 13.7% the 4 KB-block rows of size_sweep_result.txt appear to show.  That
13.7% is an artifact of syncing 192 KB of buffer to move a 4 KB payload -- a 48x
amplification caused by running a small block on a kernel built for a large one.


AN EASY WIN THIS EXPOSES

Switching the hosts from bo.sync(dir) to bo.sync(dir, size, 0) costs one
argument and saves 11.3 us on every 4 KB-block call.  On the 4 KB configuration
that is 147.0 -> 135.7 us of offload time, i.e. 27.86 -> 30.18 M sym/s including
DMA, a free 8.3%.  At a 64 KB block it saves 4.0 us on a 1245 us call (0.3%) --
not worth doing there, which is itself the point: the fix matters exactly where
the buffer is oversized relative to the payload.

Not applied here.  It changes the measurement boundary of every number already
recorded in this project, so it should be a deliberate, separately-recorded
change rather than something folded into a sweep.
