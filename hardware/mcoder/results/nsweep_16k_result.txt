Block-size sweep — mcoder K=8 at MC_MAX_IN=16384, ON FABRIC (KV260 PL @ 200 MHz)
Rebuilt kernel, 4x the block of the shipped design.  Same coder, same K, same
clock; only the staging arrays grew.

Why a rebuild was needed: MC_MAX_IN sizes the buf[][]/cout[][] staging arrays,
so it is compiled into the RTL.  The shipped 4 KB bitstream clamps any larger
request host-side and cannot be swept past 4095 no matter what the host asks.

Build: synth/build_16k.sh (HLS) + synth/relink_16k_custom.sh (link).
  MC_MAX_IN=16384, MC_MAX_OUT=32768, MC_KWAY=8, MC_CTX_LUTRAM
  platform kv260_custom (VBNV xilinx_kv260_kv260_hardware_platform_0_0)
  artifacts: bin/arith_16k.bin, bin/bench_host_arm_16k

Method: bench_host_arm_16k -N <n> -n 500, chrono around kernel enqueue+wait
only -- the same boundary as onfabric_result.txt and nsweep_result.txt.
Synthetic 'a'+(i%7) payload.  Every point verified lossless.


RESOURCES — the block grew 4x, the design barely did

                          4 KB (shipped)   16 KB (this)
  BRAM                    42 (14%)         58 (20%)
  LUT                     43165 (36%)      43165 (36%)
  FF                      13170 (5%)       13170 (5%)
  DSP                     0                0
  HLS estimated Fmax      --               228.31 MHz
  post-route WNS          +0.754 ns        +0.243 ns
  failing endpoints       0 of 71960       0 of 71966
  kernel clock            200 MHz          200 MHz (clk_out2, 5.000 ns)

LUT and FF are byte-identical: the coder is untouched and only the staging
arrays grew.  BRAM went up by 16 blocks, not by 4x -- HLS packs the larger
arrays into BRAM18/36 far more efficiently than the 4 KB ones, which wasted
most of each block.  Timing still closes, with less margin (+0.243 vs +0.754),
consistent with the post-route critical path living in the Concat output copy
loop -- exactly what a 4x longer block stretches.

Least-squares fit  t_call = t_fixed + N * t_sym:

  kernel only    t_fixed = 56.77 us   t_sym = 17.85 ns   R2 = 0.99983
  kernel + DMA   t_fixed = 69.52 us   t_sym = 17.99 ns   R2 = 0.99995

  asymptotic throughput   56.0 M sym/s
  fixed == kernel cost at N ~ 3.2 kB
  fixed share at N=16384  16.2%  (was 44% at N=4095)


FINDING 1 — the 4 KB fit predicted this to within 0.8%.

nsweep_result.txt fitted the shipped 4 KB bitstream and extrapolated beyond its
own measurable range.  Those extrapolations can now be checked:

    N        predicted        measured        error
   8192   205.30 us (39.90)  201.82 (40.59)   -1.69%
  12288   279.02 us (44.04)  275.85 (44.55)   -1.14%
  16384   352.75 us (46.45)  349.99 (46.81)   -0.78%

The error shrinks with N and the model is slightly conservative throughout.
The two independent fits also agree on both parameters:

                    4 KB build     16 KB build
    t_fixed         57.85 us       56.77 us      (1.9% apart)
    t_sym           18.00 ns       17.85 ns      (0.8% apart)

Two separate bitstreams, two separate board sessions, one reboot in between.
That is the strongest evidence in this project that the linear per-call model is
real and not a curve-fitting artifact -- and it retroactively justifies quoting
the 4 KB fit's asymptote.


FINDING 2 — the same block size costs the same, so the bigger kernel is free.

At N=4095 the 16 KB bitstream measures 131.08 us against the 4 KB bitstream's
129.4-131.6 us across three sessions.  Identical within run-to-run scatter.
Carrying 4x the staging capacity costs nothing when you do not use it, so there
is no reason to keep a 4 KB build around except as the documented artifact.


FINDING 3 — compression also improves with block size.

  N=4095   41.20%      N=8192   39.40%      N=16384  38.51%

A 6.5% relative gain in ratio, for free, on the same coder.  The mechanism is
the K-way split: at K=8 a 4095-byte block gives 512-byte chunks, and every chunk
restarts its context model from MC_CTX_INIT.  A 16384-byte block gives 2048-byte
chunks, so the adaptation cost is amortised over 4x more symbols.

This is the same effect how_it_works.md documents as the *penalty* for raising K
(+7.7 points from K=1 to K=16), read in the other direction: raising the block
size at fixed K is the free version of that trade.  Throughput and ratio move
together here, which is unusual and worth stating plainly.


FINDING 4 — DMA remains a fixed cost.

  intercept  56.77 -> 69.52 us  (+12.8 us)
  slope      17.85 -> 17.99 ns  (+0.14 ns/sym, +0.8%)

Same conclusion as the 4 KB sweep: a payload this size is too small for transfer
time to matter; what costs is the sync pair.  The intercept delta grew from
8.6 to 12.8 us because the buffers being synced are 4x larger.


WHERE THIS LEAVES THE DESIGN

  block   M sym/s   fixed share
   4095    31.24      43%          shipped
  16384    46.81      16%          measured here, +50% throughput
    inf    56.0        0%          fit asymptote

Raising the block from 4 KB to 16 KB buys +50% throughput and -2.7 points of
compressed size, for 16 BRAM and 0.5 ns of timing margin.  The coder itself was
not touched.

A previous note in nsweep_result.txt projected ~58% BRAM at 16 KB and declared
26 KB infeasible at ~95%.  Both figures were wrong -- they assumed the staging
arrays scale linearly into BRAM, and the measurement says 20% at 16 KB.  On this
evidence 32 KB is likely to fit; the real ceiling is the container header, which
carries rlen/clen as uint16 and therefore caps a chunk at 64 KB.  That is worth
one more rebuild if the throughput matters.

Reproduce:
  cd synth && bash build_16k.sh          # HLS + first link
  bash relink_16k_custom.sh              # link against kv260_custom
  scp ../bin/arith_16k.bin $BOARD:/tmp/
  # NOTE: keep exactly ONE .bin in /lib/firmware/xilinx/mcoder/ -- xmutil fails
  # to load the app if a second .bin (e.g. a backup) is left in the slot dir.
  ssh $BOARD 'sudo cp /tmp/arith_16k.bin /lib/firmware/xilinx/mcoder/mcoder.bin
              sudo xmutil unloadapp; sudo xmutil loadapp mcoder'
  ssh $BOARD 'cd /tmp; for N in 1024 2048 4095 6144 8192 12288 16384; do
      XILINX_XRT=/usr ./bench_host_arm_16k \
          -x /lib/firmware/xilinx/mcoder/mcoder.bin -N $N -n 500; done'
